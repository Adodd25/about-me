## On Security

**Security enables, it doesn't block.** The best security is invisible. When security becomes an obstacle, people find workarounds. When security enables people to do their jobs safely, it becomes part of the culture.

**Security is a business problem.** Technical controls matter, but so does business context. The best security solutions consider user experience, organizational culture, and strategic objectives—not just threat models.

**Assume breach.** The question isn't whether you'll be compromised. It's whether you'll detect it, how fast you'll respond, and how resilient your systems are. Build for recovery, not just prevention.

**Compliance is the floor, not the ceiling.** Passing an audit doesn't mean you're secure. Real security requires going beyond checkboxes to understand actual risk.

## On Leadership

**Lift as you climb.** Success means nothing if you're not bringing others with you. Through HBCUvc and mentorship, I work to create pathways for underrepresented talent in cybersecurity and venture capital.

**Diverse perspectives make better decisions.** My unconventional path—from Kinesiology to cybersecurity—taught me that different backgrounds bring unique insights. Teams that think alike miss blind spots.

**Translate, don't intimidate.** Technical jargon alienates stakeholders. The best security leaders can explain complex risks in terms that resonate with executives, board members, and everyday users.

**Lead with questions, not answers.** The higher you go, the less you know about ground-level details. Ask questions. Listen. Then decide.

## On Career

**Non-traditional paths are valid paths.** I didn't have a CS degree. I didn't start in tech. That's okay. What matters is curiosity, work ethic, and the ability to learn.

**Depth and breadth both matter.** Go deep in one area—become an expert. But also go broad—understand how your expertise connects to business, strategy, and other domains.

**Your network is your net worth.** Not in a transactional way. In a genuine, human way. Help people. Stay in touch. The opportunities will follow.

**Bet on yourself.** I went back to school twice—once for my MS, once for my MBA. Both were risks. Both were worth it.

## On Venture & Startups

**Security is a competitive advantage.** Startups that take security seriously from day one build trust with customers, avoid costly breaches, and position themselves for enterprise sales.

**Most security advice for startups is wrong.** Startups don't need enterprise security programs. They need right-sized security that grows with them.

**The best founders listen.** Not to everyone. But to people with relevant experience. Coachability is underrated.

---

## Things I've Changed My Mind About

**"Security people are gatekeepers."** I used to think our job was to say no. Now I understand our job is to find a secure way to say yes.

**"Compliance is pointless."** I used to dismiss compliance as checkbox theater. Now I see it as a forcing function that, while imperfect, raises the baseline for everyone.

**"Technical skills are all that matter."** Early in my career, I thought the best security people were the most technical. Now I know that communication, leadership, and business acumen matter just as much—maybe more.

---

## My Operating Principles

1. **Do the work.** There are no shortcuts. Preparation beats talent when talent doesn't prepare.

2. **Stay curious.** The threat landscape changes constantly. So should your knowledge.

3. **Build relationships before you need them.** Don't network when you need something. Network all the time.

4. **Embrace the uncomfortable.** Growth happens outside your comfort zone. Pursue it.

5. **Leave things better than you found them.** Code, teams, organizations, communities.
